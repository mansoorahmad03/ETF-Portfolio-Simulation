# **7 ETF Portfolio Simulation**



##### **Background**:



This is a simulation of a group of market events that occurred in real life from 2025-2026. The simulation involves a group of 7 ETFs (exchange-traded funds) for which economic declines and business uncertainty resulted in a reverse stock split for 6 ETFs of 85% of the previous total quantity of shares on the market and 1 ETF resulting in a split to 50% of the previous total shares on the market (prior to the split).



During the second half of 2025, there was a new series of ETFs released labeled in the category: high-yield ETFs. For these ETFs, the dividend yield was sometimes as much as 100-150% for some versions, such as AMDY and MSTY. They advertised high income with limited growth and were meant to be held long-term for passive income rather than market value gains.



Investors could invest as little as $10,000 into something like AMDY or MSTY and have as much as hundreds of dollars in dividends, with a qualified dividend tax rate deducted every month, since the distribution frequency was weekly for some.



However, due to the nature of the investment strategy, the share values dropped considerably for certain high-yield ETFs, specifically MSTY, which is a sister ETF to AMDY. MSTY's strategy involved calling shorts and put options against the parent stock MSTR, which represents the company MicroStrategy. Only when there were massive volatility swings, either in a price increase or a price decrease in MSTR, was income generated as dividends for these types of securities (for example, AMDY has the same strategy against AMD Corporation).



In addition, the underlying asset for some of these ETFs (such as MSTY) was completely held in Bitcoin for which there was volatility due to it being an e-currency. Drops in Bitcoin's value also affected these ETFs.



Any time there was a volatility swing in the underlying stock (MSTR), there were large distributions to investors who held the ETF MSTY. There were also swings in the value of MSTY every week during the weekly distributions. Prior to distributions, as investors anticipated earnings for the week, the share values rose. After the distributions, the share values would drop for a day or two due to the fact that almost the entire earnings based on shorts and put options were immediately distributed.



In addition, each time this occurred, the net assets would drop, resulting in NAV erosion of the ETF. This caused MSTY to slowly decline week by week in value. By December 2025, a 4-to-1 reverse stock split was declared. There were then 25% of the original number of shares, and the closing price the day before the split occurred was used to increase the new security's value by 4 times.



Eventually, as 2026 rolled in, business uncertainty due to presidential legislation, along with further declines due to the declaration of war during the US-Iran conflict, the values of MSTY began to decline to levels of market values even lower than before the reverse share split. It was almost as if it were a penny stock.



AMDY is a sister ETF to MSTY and is dependent on the swings of AMD Corporation. However, the concept is the same, and at any point the values can decline to a point where the fund managers decide to issue a reverse-share split to pass the illusion to new investors that the ETF has a high value, not similar to a penny stock.



Though the other six ETFs are typical ETFs with not such a high dividend yield and no history of reverse-share splits, and concentrated on other markets (since ETFs hold a pool of investments in various industries unlike stocks), their values still declined along with various other stocks or ETFs in the NASDAQ and US securities market.



To make the simulation more challenging for the model, it was specified that a reverse-share split (or reverse stock split for non-ETFs) occurred to a lesser extent for the 6 ETFs besides AMDY, with AMDY having a large-impact reverse share split. In addition, similar to MSTY, it was also specified that share values declined to levels below the prior market value (just like MSTY) to further the complexity of the task. However, both drops in value and reverse-share splits are at the same percentage.



For example, MSTY has 50% of the previous number of shares while also having a market value drop of 50% of its previous amount. That is the essence of this task.

# ETF-Portfolio-Simulation
This is code for a simulation of economic decline that resulted in declining share values for 7 ETFs, with two ETFs specifically going through a reverse-share split based on historical events that occurred during the end of FY 2025 and onwards. Some of the calculations are different than occured during 2025-2026 for the purpose of showing the drastic effects of certain ETF strategies (high-yield investments).
